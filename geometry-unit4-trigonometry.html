Foundational Rule

1. Pythagorean Theorem & Common Triples

For any right triangle with legs a and b and hypotenuse c (the longest side across from the 90° angle):

The Core Formula
a² + b² = c²

Leg² + Leg² = Hypotenuse²

Right Triangle Ratios

2. SOH CAH TOA Breakdown

Trigonometric ratios relate the acute angle θ (theta) of a right triangle to its side lengths. Label your triangle relative to θ first:

S O H

Sine (sin)

sin(θ) = Opp / Hyp

Opposite side divided by Hypotenuse.

C A H

Cosine (cos)

cos(θ) = Adj / Hyp

Adjacent side divided by Hypotenuse.

T O A

Tangent (tan)

tan(θ) = Opp / Adj

Opposite side divided by Adjacent side.

📍 How to Solve Any Trig Problem in 4 Steps:

  1. Identify Reference Angle (θ): Circle the given or target acute angle.
  2. Label Sides: Hypotenuse (across 90°), Opposite (across θ), Adjacent (next to θ).
  3. Pick SOH, CAH, or TOA: Choose the ratio containing your known and unknown values.
  4. Solve Equation: Use inverse trig (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹) when finding a missing angle!
Exact Radical Rules

3. Special Right Triangles

These two special right triangles appear constantly on the SAT, ACT, and geometry final exams. Memorize their exact side ratio patterns:

45° - 45° - 90° Triangle

Isosceles Right

Both legs are equal in length, and the hypotenuse is leg × √2.

Leg 1 = x
Leg 2 = x
Hypotenuse = x√2
💡 Example: If leg = 5, hypotenuse = 5√2. If hypotenuse = 10, leg = 10 / √2 = 5√2.

30° - 60° - 90° Triangle

Scalene Right

Everything builds from the Short Leg (x) across from the 30° angle.

Short Leg (opp 30°) = x
Long Leg (opp 60°) = x√3
Hypotenuse (opp 90°) = 2x
💡 Example: If short leg = 4, long leg = 4√3, and hypotenuse = 8.
